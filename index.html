About

I’m Julia, a full-stack software engineer in Austin. I studied Computer Science with a minor in Graphic Design at Texas A&M, and I still approach software the way I approach a canvas: get the structure right first, then make it feel good to use.

Right now I work at BitKernel on Goose, a SaaS platform for live and on-demand video that runs on web, Android, iOS and smart TVs. I build Java/Spring Boot backend services, fix bugs in our TypeScript web SDK, and track down problems wherever they turn up, from Redis queues to native crash logs. I also review a lot of my teammates’ code, and I run the tests myself instead of just reading the diff. That habit has caught some sneaky bugs before they shipped.

Outside of work, I build things I want to exist. DingDuff is a free AI legal research tool I’m building with friends. It pulls U.S. case law from CourtListener and uses a series of LLM steps to filter and summarize long court opinions into something lawyers can actually use. Nightly is an iOS app for nightly reflection that I designed, built and launched on the App Store by myself, and Applied is a job application tracker I built to learn what actually leads to better outcomes in a job search. In school I helped build a clinical decision tool for forensic nurses, which is where I learned how much careful design matters when people are under pressure.

When I’m not coding, I draw, paint and spend time in my garden (Plant Life is a little love letter to that). Art shapes how I think about hierarchy, spacing and atmosphere on a screen. Across everything I make, the goal is the same: software that is reliable and calm, and helps people feel a little more grounded.
